## Skips

- Newton's law of cooling (classes 01-04): a cooling mug with a thermometer takes 20+ min to show an exponential; a pre-recorded data plot does the job, no live demo worth the minutes.
- Population growth / separation of variables (03-04): no physical demo; a dice/coin-decay simulation exists but is a 15-min activity, not a demo.
- Class 08 (Jupyter ODEs) and quiz days (07, 18): no demo by design.
- Complex numbers basics (11): nothing physical beyond the turntable two-peg question (multiplying by i rotates by 90 deg), which belongs on 12.
- Maclaurin / Taylor series (14-15): no "Taylor machine" apparatus found in any catalog; partial-sum plots in Python (ipywidgets slider on jupyterhub) beat any physical stand-in. The pendulum race's theta0^2/16 term is the physical anchor.
- Matrix multiplication, identity, inverse (19): nothing physical that beats a drawn grid; the rubber sheet is the only candidate and it fits 20 better.
- Determinants (20): only as the area-scale bonus inside rubber-sheet-eigenvectors.
- Damped/driven resonance as complex ODE (12): belongs to PHY 317's forced SHO days; in 210 the RC phasor is the cheaper version.
