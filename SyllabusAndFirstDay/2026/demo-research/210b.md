## Skips

- **Class 22-23 (setting up 1D/2D integrals, Cartesian 2D integrals):** no demo that beats a well-chosen board problem. "Balance an odd shape to find its center of mass" (plumb-line method) is intro mechanics; the integral never shows up.
- **Class 24 (polar coordinates):** looked at "balance a semicircular plate to find its CM at 4R/(3 pi)". It is a real effect but a weak prediction (students just guess "a bit below the middle"), and the integral is the content.
- **Pappus's theorems:** cute, but no physical demo shows anything a sketch does not.
- **Class 25 (line and surface integrals):** nothing found that is better than a picture. "Work along two paths with friction" is intro mechanics, and it belongs to conservative fields anyway.
- **Divergence / flux (classes 29-31):** dye or smoke from a point source in a tank is a picture of a source, not a measurement of div. Too much setup for a point that a vector-field plot makes in seconds.
- **Divergence theorem and Stokes' theorem (class 32):** no genuine physical demo found. Any "flux out = sources inside" demo is just the divergence picture again. The closest real thing is the vortex demo above (the circulation around the drain is nonzero even though curl = 0 outside the core), so cite it here instead of adding a new demo.
- **Conservative fields (class 33):** nothing worth the minutes. The physical examples (gravity path independence, the drain-vortex circulation) are already covered by earlier demos, and the topological point (curl = 0 but not conservative on a punctured plane) is the vortex again, best made on the board referring back to it.
- **Heat equation, generally (class 37):** apart from the heat-front entry (only if a dry run is clean), nothing is quick and quantitative. A computed heat-equation animation in the notebook is more reliable than any hardware.
